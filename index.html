PROCESSING NOTICE | USGS 3DEP

This calculation uses the USGS 3DEP ImageServer. Multipoint sampling requests 48 preselected geographic positions per radial in one query. Results can still take a minute or longer, depending on USGS response times.

Fastest uncached full scan: 12 USGS queries (12 bearings × 48 requested positions = 576 terrain positions), in four chunks of three. Full 24- or 36-bearing scans require 24 or 36 queries. Repeated scans may reuse cached results, and missing data may require extra elevation lookups. Multipoint requests are preferred, with legacy polyline fallback when the route is unavailable. Method & assumptions

Distance from both antenna heights

Heights hₜ and hᵣ are in feet AGL; distances are in miles.

Radio: d ≈ 1.415 × (√hₜ + √hᵣ)
Geometric: d ≈ 1.225 × (√hₜ + √hᵣ)

The calculation uses √(2kRh) for each endpoint, R = 6,371 km, and k = 4/3 for a standard refracting atmosphere or k = 1 for geometric line of sight. Coefficients shown are rounded; calculations use the constants directly. See ITU horizon geometry.

A useful first approximation

Area = πd², a theoretical circle in square miles. Radiation center AMSL = base elevation AMSL + radiation center AGL.

Assumes a smooth Earth and similar ground elevations at both ends. These heights are not HAAT (height above average terrain). Base elevation is not added to the horizon height.

The 8 m antenna setback is a rough convenience estimate, not FCC antenna data. Enter the actual radiation center when known. Geocoded addresses and ZIP centers are approximations, not surveyed tower sites. Population and density are reserved for a future data integration.

Engineering reality check: The Earth keeps curving, no matter how inconvenient that is for the spreadsheet. And a clear terrain path is still not a guarantee that the RF signal will cooperate.
