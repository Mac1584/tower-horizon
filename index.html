03 / Assumptions & formulas

AGL means above ground level; AMSL means above mean sea level. Transmitter height hₜ = overall tower height − the editable top offset. Receiver height hᵣ = feet × 0.3048. Both heights in the formulas below are in meters.

Geometric distance: d = √(2R / 1000) × (√hₜ + √hᵣ)

Standard radio distance: d = √(2kR / 1000) × (√hₜ + √hᵣ)

R = 6,371 km; k = 4/3; d in km. Coefficients are approximately 3.57 and 4.12.

Theoretical area: A = πd², using the standard radio distance as a circular radius. Radiation center AMSL: base elevation + hₜ.

These are small-height, smooth spherical-Earth approximations with a common ground reference. Standard refraction is represented by an effective Earth radius of 4/3 the physical radius. The distances include both the transmitter and receiver horizons. Base elevation is not terrain clearance or height above average terrain (HAAT).

Terrain, buildings, foliage, frequency, transmitter power, antenna patterns, interference, and changing atmospheric refraction are excluded. The circle can include water and obstructed land. Population and density require geographic Census data and an explicit counting method; no estimate is invented here. This educational tool is not an engineering study or an FCC filing tool.
